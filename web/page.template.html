Single-Press Keypad

Loading word model…

Press each key once per letter. 0 is a space, 1 deletes (on the keyboard, holding ⌫ deletes a word at a time), and * swaps the selected word for the next guess, and holding it finishes the word you're typing. # is punctuation: the model picks . , ! or ?, and between letters it's an apostrophe. Apostrophes are optional, so "dont" also gives "don't". Hold any number key to type that digit (hold 1 for "1", hold 0 for "0"). Digits end the word, so hold 4 then type 3837 for "4ever". # next to digits makes money and decimals: # 6 # 000 # 00 gives "$6,000.00". For call signs, spell them once in exact mode (ke7aqa) and send: after that one press per character works, 5 3 7 2 7 2, with the 7 tapped or held.

Hold # for exact spelling, for names and words the model doesn't know. Press a key repeatedly to cycle its letters and then its digit (2 = a, b, c, 2), and pause or press another key to move on. # cycles . , ? ! ' : and 0 gives a space, or the digit 0 on a second press. On QWERTY, exact mode types the letter you tap. Hold # again to go back to predictive.

Capitals are automatic: sentence starts, I, names, places, brands and apps with their usual capitalization (iPhone, McDonald's, YouTube), the words around a word ("in May" but "I may", "Will Smith"), and call signs (KE7AQA). For a word it learned from you, tap it and Aa to capitalize it from then on.

A number that could be a time offers both: 5 # 30 is 5.30 on its own and 5:30 after at, by or until, or with am or pm after it. Tap it to switch.

Send copies the text, clears it and learns from it: the words you use and the words you use together get more likely, and a word you spelled exactly, or typed on QWERTY when no word matched, is guessed from its keys next time. That covers punctuation too. The next message also starts with your last few in mind, so words you just used are likelier. Once you start a new sentence, the one before it stays as it is. What it learns stays in this browser, kept for each model separately. Forget clears it.

Tutorial (next to the title) walks through all of this in a few steps.

Tap any word to see other words with the same keys. Picking one locks it, and the rest of the sentence re-decodes around it.

The QWERTY keyboard sends only each letter's keypad number (shown in the corner), so the model decodes it exactly as if you'd used the keypad. When no word fits, it keeps the letters you tapped. Keys grow and shrink in place with how likely each letter is to come next. A computer keyboard works the same way, and Tab finishes the word.

Neural (Phone model) adds a small neural network that reads everything typed before each word, not just the last two words. It's downloaded once next to the page and runs here too.
